# On Romanticizing The Process
I just bought a new keyboard. It's not the nicest thing but it makes an amazing sound. 10 years ago it used to be in the that the best sound your keyboard could make was this immense click. The sound was supposed to be reminiscent of older typewriters and keyboards but to be candid it sounded plasticy. As the years have gone on keyboard fans have grown more and more fond of a muted, deep sound descibed most accurately as a *thock*. I've been into keyboards for a long time so I had a couple laying around that had [[broken keys I later fixed]] but before I figured out I could do that I ordered a new one and got to work. 

I bought a new keyboard the other day. I can't say it's made me smarter or more productive but it's made me enjoy being at my desk more. It makes me look forward to sitting down and typing. 10 years ago keyboard fanatics regarded the clear, plasticy *click* as the best noise a keyboard could make; today the most sought-after noise is the *thock*, a deeper, milder sound that can be found in 100s of keyboard demo videos. I got a thocky keyboard, and it gives me great joy to type on it. 

Recently I started using an old fountain pen I have had around for years. It's a tiny lipstick pen with the name "Ellen" engraved on it. I think it's from the 30s if my dating is right. It has a pump fill which was completely new to me when I first got the pen. It writes like a dream even after almost 100 years. But does it make my handwriting better, or my thoughts clearer? Of course not, but it helps me get the ball rolling.
